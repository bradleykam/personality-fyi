Enneagram vs MBTI: The 9 Types and Why MBTI Predicts More

Frameworks · Updated 2026-06-16 · By Brad Kam

The Enneagram and MBTI are the two most popular personality systems. The Enneagram sorts people into 9 types by core motivation. MBTI sorts people into 16 types by how they think and decide. If you only have time to use one to actually make decisions about your career and relationships, this page explains why we lead with MBTI - and gives you the 9 Enneagram types and how they map onto MBTI.

The 9 Enneagram types

The Enneagram describes nine core motivations. Each is defined by a basic desire and a basic fear. Here they are at a glance, with the MBTI types they most commonly overlap with.

What each system actually measures

The two systems answer different questions:

  • The Enneagram measures why - your core motivation and the fear underneath it. It is introspective and motivation-first.
  • MBTI measures how - how you take in information (Sensing vs Intuition), make decisions (Thinking vs Feeling), direct your energy (Introvert vs Extravert), and structure your life (Judging vs Perceiving).

Because they measure different layers, you can be both at once - for example, an Enneagram 5 who is also an INTJ. They are not competitors so much as different lenses.

Why we lead with MBTI

If the goal is to make real decisions - what work fits you, who you get along with - MBTI is the more useful tool, for a simple reason: cognitive preferences show up in observable behavior more reliably than core motivations do.

Your MBTI type predicts how you'll communicate, what kind of work will drain you, and which other types you'll click with. Those are things you can act on this week. The Enneagram's core-motivation model is valuable for inner work, but it is harder to validate, harder to type accurately, and less directly connected to the practical questions of careers and compatibility that most people are actually trying to answer.

That is why every tool on personality.fyi - career planning, compatibility, the type guides - is built on MBTI. If you only learn one framework well, learn the one that maps to the decisions in front of you.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the Enneagram and MBTI?

The Enneagram sorts people into 9 types based on core motivation and fear (why you do things). MBTI sorts people into 16 types based on cognitive preferences (how you take in information and make decisions). MBTI maps more directly to observable behavior, career fit, and compatibility, which is why we lead with it.

Is the Enneagram or MBTI better?

They measure different things, so neither is strictly 'better.' But for practical use - predicting which careers and which people you'll fit - MBTI is more actionable, because cognitive preferences show up in behavior more reliably than core motivations do. The Enneagram is useful for inner work and motivation; MBTI is useful for decisions about work and relationships.

Can you be both an Enneagram type and an MBTI type?

Yes. They are independent systems. You might be an Enneagram 5 and an INTJ, for example. Certain combinations are more common than others, but any Enneagram number can pair with any MBTI type.
